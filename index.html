Undergraduate individual Capstone project (University of Moratuwa) developing an autonomous alternating-grip climbing robot designed to ascend tapered, inclined coconut palms reliably and non-invasively. Synthesized a novel 5-DOF mechanical architecture that adapts dynamically to changing trunk diameters and inclinations while guaranteeing fall-safe stability.
Kinematic Type 5-DOF Alternating Gait
Taper Adaptation Ø 80 mm – 150 mm
Step / Stride 150 mm / Cycle (Smax=250mm)
Safety Margin Fail-Safe Clamping Interlock

💡 Key Engineering Contributions:

  • Active Diameter Sensing & Articulated Grippers: Modeled multi-tier gear-driven curved clamp grippers in SolidWorks 2024 with integrated proximity/ToF distance sensors. Engineered a pre-grasp scanning routine that contracts the gripper incrementally to detect bark boundaries, calculating exact local trunk diameter (80 mm – 150 mm) before applying calibrated clamping torque.
  • Closed-Loop Trunk Inclination Adaptation: Developed a dynamic tilt-correction algorithm utilizing upper and lower distance sensors on each detached gripper. When ascending inclined trunks, the system measures the differential distance offset and commands an articulated pitch joint to rotate the gripper plane parallel to the trunk surface prior to locking.
  • Fail-Safe 6-Phase Gait State Machine: Designed a sensor-triggered Finite State Machine (FSM) controlling sequential climbing: Anchored $\rightarrow$ Upper Release $\rightarrow$ Body Linear Extension $\rightarrow$ Diameter-Adaptive Upper Reclamp $\rightarrow$ Lower Release $\rightarrow$ Body Retraction $\rightarrow$ Lower Reclamp. Strictly enforces that at least one gripper unit maintains verified holding force before the other releases.
Capstone Engineering Progress: Completed mathematical kinematic modeling, free-body static equilibrium derivations, and parametric SolidWorks 3D CAD assemblies; currently conducting multi-body dynamic simulation and stress analysis to optimize actuator torque profiles ahead of physical fabrication.
